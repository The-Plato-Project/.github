
# Mission Statement

Our mission is to restore the art of reading Plato as a living encounter between human soul and the Greek text, where understanding is grounded in the original Greek and guided by the reader's own questions, attention, and discernment.

We seek to create an environment in which reading becomes an active pursuit of understanding rather than the passive reception of established interpretations. By attending closely to Plato's language, its nuances, recurring patterns, and connections across the dialogues, readers are free to follow what emerges from the text, question their assumptions, and move their consciousness beyond the surface and shadows of things.

Technology has a deliberate but subordinate role in this pursuit. It provides access to the texts, linguistic evidence, and relationships that readers may wish to investigate, without determining their meaning or directing their conclusions. In this project AI is only used to surface pointers and references, known data about the text and the language of Plato. In no way do we use AI to interpret meaning or create argument.

At the heart of this project is the conviction that human understanding and the wisdom of the soul cannot be delegated to the techne of the machine. The reader must remain the one who is ever present, as one who encounters, questions, remembers, discerns, and ultimately comes to see something they didn't notice before. Our purpose is to preserve and cultivate that distinctly human work.


# AI, Ignorance, and the Source of Human Creativity

## The Philosophy Behind Chora and the Plato Project

The Plato Project is developing a new approach to the study of Plato's dialogues and Ancient Greek through Chora, an environment for close reading, exploration, and independent inquiry.

Artificial intelligence is part of this undertaking, but not in the conventional sense of building a system that teaches readers what Plato means.

Instead, Chora integrates AI as an interlocutor: a participant in the reader's inquiry, capable of bringing attention to words, passages, relationships, and possibilities without assuming authority over their meaning.

This approach grows out of a larger philosophy about technology, human understanding, and the creative possibilities of not knowing.

The following principles guide our work.

## The Purpose Must Come Before the Agent

AI is most powerful when it is integrated into something specific and meaningful. Not when it exists as an abstract application that asks us to invent a purpose for it.

**The purpose must come before the agent.**

We should not begin by asking what an agent can do. We should begin with something we deeply understand, something we care about, something we are genuinely engaged in exploring.

AI becomes powerful when it enters a domain we know in our bones. Not necessarily because we are experts, but because we have an intimate relationship with the work itself.

## The Limits of Expertise

I am not an expert on Plato. And the beautiful thing is, nobody is.

Plato's dialogues are not objects to be mastered, reduced to conclusions, or exhausted by explanation. They remain open to inquiry.

**We are not simply developing better tools. We are discovering an entirely different way of interacting with the world.**

Agents are not here to make us better than we are. Nor is their greatest purpose to outperform us, replace our thinking, or become authorities over what we do not yet understand.

The real possibility of AI is that it can transform our relationship with what we do not know.

It can help us encounter possibilities, relationships, and distinctions that might otherwise remain unseen.

## Ignorance as the Source of Discovery

**Our ignorance is not a deficiency to be eliminated.**

It is the very ground from which curiosity, creativity, discovery, and inquiry emerge.

The desire to know and control everything can actually limit what we are capable of seeing. When we believe we already understand something, we may stop noticing what is before us.

**When we are willing to stand in our ignorance, something extraordinary becomes possible.**

We become curious. We begin to question. We notice what we previously overlooked. We encounter possibilities we could not have anticipated.

And paradoxically, we may accomplish far more from that place of not knowing than from the pursuit of mastery and control.

## Technology in Service of Human Inquiry

**AI should not replace the human encounter with the world. It should deepen it.**

The technology should help us become more fully engaged in our own inquiry, rather than remove us from the experience of discovery.

This is what we are building with the Plato Project.

Not an artificial expert on Plato, but an environment in which a human reader and an artificial interlocutor can participate in an ongoing inquiry, grounded in the text, without pretending that either possesses the final answer.
